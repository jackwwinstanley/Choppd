# exp-001-overhead-vs-hero

**Question (one line):** for pasta phase-1 POT-CONTENT steps, does a
top-down overhead read faster at glance size than a 45° hero angle?

**One dimension varied:** camera angle ONLY. Subject (butter foaming in
the pot), lighting, cookware, surface, and framing language are held
byte-constant across variants. (The question as asked was single-
dimension — no split needed.)

## VARIANTS

**A — top-down overhead:**
> Top-down overhead photograph looking straight into a large stainless
> pot on a stovetop: butter fully melted and foaming pale gold, bubbly
> white foam, zero browning. Bright even neutral daylight, neutral white
> balance, no colored shadows, one subject filling the frame, light
> neutral surface, no garnish, no props, no text, realistic home-kitchen
> result. Square.

**B — 45° hero angle:**
> 45-degree angled photograph into a large stainless pot on a stovetop:
> butter fully melted and foaming pale gold, bubbly white foam, zero
> browning. Bright even neutral daylight, neutral white balance, no
> colored shadows, one subject filling the frame, light neutral surface,
> no garnish, no props, no text, realistic home-kitchen result. Square.

## N per variant: 4

## File naming contract
`results/exp-A-1.png` … `exp-A-4.png`, `exp-B-1.png` … `exp-B-4.png`

## Judging criteria (what "winning" means)
1. **Thumbnail glance test (primary):** at ~1-inch size, which variant
   makes the foam-vs-browning state readable fastest?
2. Surface visibility: is the full foam surface (the diagnostic feature)
   visible, or does the pot wall occlude it?
3. Consistency potential: which angle will reproduce most consistently
   across the other pot states (boil, simmer, sauce)?
Winner = the variant that takes 2 of 3, judged by the founder on the
downloaded files.

the winning PROMPT gets regenerated via /generate-images.
